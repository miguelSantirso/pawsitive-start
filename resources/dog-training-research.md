# Dog-training research brief (verified sources, 2026-11-08)

Compiled for Pawsitive Start. Facts below were checked against the sources in the table; soft/unverified claims are flagged.

## 1. Principles of humane, effective training
- **Positive reinforcement**: add something the dog values immediately after a behavior; behavior becomes more likely. Train "what TO do", reinforce calm alternatives.
- **Markers**: a word ("Yes!") or clicker pinpoints the instant the dog got it right, reward within ~1s. Clicker vs. word: no meaningful difference — consistency is what counts (clickertraining.com FAQ; KPA).
- **Timing**: mark the behavior at the instant it happens; treat for that behavior.
- **Rate of reinforcement**: new skills → ~8–10 tiny treats/min (trainer guidance, soft). If 6 misses in a row, lower difficulty.
- **Treats**: pea-sized even for large dogs; high-value food (cheese/chicken) for big moments; keep treats ≲10% of daily calories (common vet guidance, soft).
- **Sessions**: 5 short daily sessions beat one long grind. 1–3 min for new/fragile skills, ≤5 min for confident dogs. Always end on a win.
- **Proofing — the three D's** (AKC): Duration → Distance → Distraction, one D at a time; drop the other two while adding a new one; return to the dog before releasing.
- **Management vs training**: arrange the environment so mistakes can't be rehearsed (gates, pens, supervision); training replaces management over time. For rescues, management IS the decompression plan.

## 2. Why aversive/"alpha" methods are out
- AVSAB (2021, reaffirmed): "only reward-based training methods… aversive methods (e-collars, prong, choke, leash corrections and other physical or psychological punishment) should not be used under any circumstances."
- Documented fallout: increased fear-related aggression (Vieira de Castro 2020; Herron 2008 — confrontational methods frequently elicited aggressive responses), avoidance/learned helplessness, lower engagement, lasting stress signals.
- Dominance theory refuted by modern wolf research (packs are families) and BC SPCA position statement; force/"alpha rolls" are unfair and unsafe, especially for stressed rescues.

## 3. The rescue context
### 3-3-3 rule (ASPCA Pro — guideline, not stopwatch)
- **Days 1–3 decompress**: boredom, predictability, quiet space; NO forcing contact, visitors, dog parks, intense outings, bath/test situations.
- **Weeks 1–3(4–21) settle**: personality shows; light, heavily-rewarded training begins (name, come, wait); keep kind, consistent boundaries; no flooding with events.
- **Months 1–3(4–12) belong**: trust deepens, habits set; keep ritual + training; don't assume week-3 personality is final.
- **Decompression duration varies**: days–weeks–months; common two-week shutdown framework (practitioner guidance, not formally studied).

### First weeks: what to avoid (unless medically needed)
Dog parks, pet stores, crowds/festivals, parades of visitors, forced resident-pet meetings, baths unless necessary, long hard walks. Replace with: potty drills, home sniffing games, scatter-feeding, sleep, watching household life from a safe spot.

### Trust building
- Let the dog approach you: sit sideways, no looming; soft brief eye contact only.
- **Consent test (pet–pause–pause again)**: 3–5s stroke on chest/shoulder → withdraw hand → dog leans in/nudges = continue; turns away/lip-lick/freeze = stop. Repeat forever. No pat-pat on the head.
- **Start buttons**: e.g. chin-rest consent for handling (VetPartner).
- **Stress signals** (VCA): pacing, trembling, panting, yawning, lip licking, whale eye, pinned ears, tucked tail, "suddenly not hungry", hiding, excessive sniffing. Happy = loose, wiggly, sweeping tail (stiff wag ≠ happy), take treats readily.
- Don't over-read single signals; read whole body.

### Common rescue problems + first responses
- **House-training**: assume un-trained. Out hourly when awake + after meals/sniffs/wake/play, on lead to one spot, praise+treat while still outside; supervise/pen between trips; extend freedom slowly; enzyme cleaner; NEVER punish (dogs learn to hide it).
- **Shutdown/fear**: remove all pressure; scatter tiny food; sit at distance; never drag out of hiding; vet if not eating 24h+.
- **Separation risk** (ASPCA): varies your presence, calm greetings/goodbyes, "pretend departures" (keys+coat → sit back down), long-lasting food toys only at absences; graduated-absence desensitization; pro help (CAAB/DACVB/CPDT) for moderate-severe cases; distress usually peaks in first 40 min.
- **Food guarding**: manage first (quiet corner, no hovering, never grab bowl); ASPCA protocol = approach at distance → toss treat → step closer across meals → touch bowl + better treat → lift bowl + treat; if freeze/hard stare/fast eating appears — stop DIY, hire pro. Never punish growling (removes your warning light).
- **Leash reactivity**: find threshold distance where dog can still think; mark+feed every glance at trigger ("other dogs make great things happen"); no forced greetings; dog parks off-limits post-adoption.

### Health / first vet visit (within days)
Records + fresh stool sample; vaccination status; fecal test (parasites very common); heartworm test/prevention per region; microchip registered to you; treat-based calm vet visits; spay/neuter timing varies (small ~6 months; large breeds later, vet-guided AAHA 9–15 months).

## 4. Frameworks to borrow structure from
- **AKC STAR Puppy (20 items)**: front-loads owner responsibility + handling acceptance before obedience (tolerate collar, treat/toy exchange, allow exam ears/feet, come from 5 ft...).
- **AKC CGC (10 items)**: everyday-manners map — greet stranger, sit for petting, grooming acceptance, loose walk, crowd, sit/down/stay, 10-ft recall, calm around a dog, distraction recovery, 3-min supervised separation.
- **Dunbar Open Paw (4 levels)**: level 0 → prove skills → next; classical conditioning of humans first; hand-feeding as currency; "sit for everything" settles kennel behavior.
- **Karen Pryor Academy**: marker-charging, name game, targeting, shaping, stays with release cues, loose leash; game-based micro-lessons with measurable criteria.
- **Karen Overall Relaxation Protocol**: 15-day, day-numbered ladder from "sit 5s" to complex distractions — the backbone of mat-settle & alone-time training.
- **AbsoluteDogs (commercial)**: 3-minute game concepts — borrowable format only.

## 5. Course syllabus (33 exercises / 6 stages)
Ordering logic: Stage 1 = safety/routine (nothing demanded of the dog); Stage 2 = shared language (marker, name, touch, consent) built on hand-feeding; Stage 3 = operating instructions indoors (sit, down, mat-settle, recall, leave-it, trade); Stage 4 = same skills proven outside in slices (gear, loose leash, doors, sniffari, pass-dogs); Stage 5 = the keeper-skills (crate, alone-time, quiet-on-cue, consent handling, bowl safety); Stage 6 = reliability + joy (long-line recall, tricks, guest manners, car, graduation plan/CGC).

**Stage 1 – Welcome Home (days 1–3):** safe-space-setup; do-nothing-sitting; toilet-routine; scatter-hand-feed.
**Stage 2 – Building Trust (days 3–14):** marker-word; name-game; hand-target; consent-check; gentle-mouth; signal-spotting.
**Stage 3 – First Words (weeks 2–5):** lured-sit; lured-down; mat-settle; indoor-recall; food-leave-it; trade-and-drop.
**Stage 4 – Out in the World (weeks 4–10):** gear-comfort; leash-zen (indoor); street-loose-leash; door-wait; sniffari; calm-greet.
**Stage 5 – Calm & Confidence (weeks 6–12):** crate-confidence; alone-staircase; quiet-on-cue; touch-and-vet-prep; bowl-and-bones.
**Stage 6 – Growing Together (months 3–6+):** long-line-recall; spin-trick; high-five; guest-doors; car-calm; graduation-plan.

Each exercise: goal → why (rescue angle) → 5–8 steps → 2–3 mastery criteria → mistakes → troubleshooting tip; ~30 min/week total per skill.

## 7. Top dos & don'ts
**Do:** safe space before arrival; written toilet schedule; hand-feed/scatter-feed first weeks; mark within 1 second; mini-sessions ending on wins; long line for outdoor recalls; read body language and add distance early; manage while training catches up; vet week 1 (+stool sample); log progress and celebrate small wins.
**Don't:** punish accidents/chewing/barking; alpha/dominance methods; dog parks early; forced interactions; hover over the bowl; grab the collar of a scared dog; call the dog to something bad; repeat cues ("Come! COME!"); leave them alone for long hours in week one; rush the 3-3-3 timeline.

**Soft flags** (present as practical guidance, not science): two-week shutdown; 8–10 treats/min; treats ≤10% calories.
