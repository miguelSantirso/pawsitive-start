Meals and chews without guarding

Woven into every meal8 steps0 of 8 steps done
The goalPeople near food make dogs relax — your dog keeps eating happily while you toss treats nearby and, when ready, add a bonus to the bowl.
See all steps
Why this matters

Shelter life (scarce food, raced-for meals) makes resource guarding common — and food guarding is one of the most common adoption-return reasons. The ASPCA protocol is ounce-of-prevention: your approach always predicts a better-treat bonus, so the dog has less reason to protect dinner.

What you need

  • Their normal meal
  • High-value treats (better than the bowl!)
  • A quiet corner
  • No kids near meals

Mastery check

You have got this when all of these are true in a normal session, on a normal day.

Common mistakes

  • Never take food or bones away empty-handed to prove who's in charge — this is the exact pattern that CREATES guarding.
  • No kids near meals at any stage; bowls and bones are kid-free zones forever.
  • Never punish a growl over food — it's your early warning light; punishing it removes the light, not the fuel.

If your dog is struggling

They freeze, speed-eat, or give a hard stare when I'm nearby.

Stop the ladder there — back up two steps, and stay at the easiest level for a week. If it doesn't melt, bring in a force-free behavior pro; don't DIY-push guarding beyond this.

They already growled at someone over a bone.

Don't grab or startle — calmly walk away, let them keep the bone, and swap in Stage 3's trade game the next day; meanwhile remove bone access and manage meals. Tell the whole family, and never corner a feeding dog.

Source: ASPCA food guarding protocol
