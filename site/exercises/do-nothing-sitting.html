The daily nothing habit

10–20 minutes, twice a day6 steps0 of 6 steps done
The goalSit near your dog with zero demands, letting them study you and relax without performing.
See all steps
Why this matters

First-time owners usually rush interaction — touching, calling, playing. A guarded rescue dog needs the opposite: passive company. Slowly, your presence becomes background noise they can relax next to, which is how attachment actually starts.

What you need

  • A chair or floor spot
  • Something quiet to read

Mastery check

You have got this when all of these are true in a normal session, on a normal day.

Common mistakes

  • Don't fill the silence with commands or chatter — nothing is being asked.
  • Don't loom or initiate surprise hugs; contact is always their idea.
  • Don't use this slot to brush, wipe paws or trim → it must stay boring.

If your dog is struggling

They hover and whine at me the whole time.

Whining is often the dog asking 'what do you want?' — being predictably boring answers it. Shorten the sit to 5 minutes twice a day for a few days.

They fall asleep instantly — every time.

That is the exercise working. Falling asleep near you is a big trust milestone; keep it going.
