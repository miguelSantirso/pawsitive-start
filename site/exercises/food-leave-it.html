Leave it — ignoring things on purpose

The goal'Leave it' pulls their nose off food or objects on cue, and they turn to you instead.

Why this matters

Street-snacking is one of the top rescue dangers (unknown diets, wrappers, chicken bones). Taught with rewards rather than shouting, leave-it is a real decision your dog makes — disengage from the thing, engage with you — and that skill spreads to counters, socks and stolen snacks. One boundary: 'leave it' stops the grab before it happens; when something is already in their mouth, the next exercise — the trade game — is the one that gets it back.

What you need

  • Boring treat in one hand
  • Fancy treat in the other
  • A floor and a table to generalize

Common mistakes

  • Never let them win the 'leave-it' item 'just this once' — one success can set you back; aim for no repeats.
  • Don't scold a grab — that teaches stealing-with-eyes-open; instead reset with an easier distance.
  • Don't always pay with the very item you blocked — mix your payoffs so 'leave it' isn't 'wait, then eat it'.

If your dog is struggling

They persist on the fist forever, staring.

Pay faster — the moment the nose opens away from the hand, mark. Then slowly make the boring version harder: open slightly, cover it somewhere on the floor next.

They grabbed the sock already.

Don't tear it out — that's how guarding gets learned. Trade: show something tastier, drop it away from their mouth, and when they release, swap calmly. Then remove sock access for a month: management first.
