Why this matters

Reactivity is prevented by teaching 'other dogs predict snacks' from the farthest distance where your dog stays soft and thinking — their threshold, which for a new rescue is often 30–50 feet. Working there is the difference between a dog that doesn't react and one that suddenly does.

What you need

  • A testable distance of 30–50 feet
  • Good treats
  • The 'touch' hand target from Stage 2

Common mistakes

  • No forced meet-and-greets — neutral passing first; meet-ups are Stage 6 practice with calm, known dogs.
  • Never walk head-on at another dog — head-ons read as threat in dog body language; arc, pass at a curve.
  • Don't read lunging or barking as naughtiness; it's fear or frustration at being too close — increase distance, and it melts.

If your dog is struggling

Even 50 feet is too much.

Observe from a field or parking lot at the biggest distance there is, 5 minutes, then go home — progress is distance, not duration. Most dogs expand their comfort distance in two or three clear leaps over the first two weeks.

They bark at triggers anyway.

Don't punish the bark. Move calmly away, let them sniff, and shrink expectations: today you marked 4 glances, celebrate that. If there's no progress in two weeks, find a force-free pro.

Source: ASPCA Pro: managing leash-reactive dogs
