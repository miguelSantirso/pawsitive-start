Good to know before you start

After Loose-leash walking, indoors first: the indoor slack-leash game comes first — the same rule is much easier to learn where nothing competes for attention.

Why this matters

Outside proofing is where new rescues either learn 'walk with my human' or rehearse 'drag till it works'. Short, high-pay walks beat long drag-fights — ten good minutes a day reshape leash habits far faster than one exhausting battle.

What you need

  • Harness + lead
  • Higher-value treats than the street offers
  • The quietest route at the quietest hour

Common mistakes

  • No yank-backs or leash corrections, ever — they teach 'collar = scary', and fear teaches nothing about walking with you.
  • Don't let greetings happen mid-pull — a pulled greeting rehearses pulling.
  • Don't blame excitement on the dog being 'naughty' — they're just at their learning edge, and management (shorter walks) fixes it.

If your dog is struggling

They pull from the first step of the trip.

Front-clip harness manages pulling honestly while you retrain; keep paying the seam position. And tire the zoomies at home first — a sniff-in-the-garden warm-up helps enormously.

Too excited to eat outdoors.

Lower the bar: same quiet route, quieter hour, tastier treat bits (cheese, hot dog). Start by paying from the doorway, walking three steps, feeding, going back in. Outside is a new classroom.

They spook at buses or kids on bikes.

Pause at distance, scatter treats in the grass, let them watch while snacking, and go at their pace. If every walk is a startle-fest for two weeks straight, call in a certified force-free trainer.

Source: ASPCA Pro: managing leash-reactive dogs
