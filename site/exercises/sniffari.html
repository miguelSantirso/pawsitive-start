The decompression sniff walk

The goalA slow, roamy, long-line stroll with zero obedience demands, where your dog's nose decides the route.

Why this matters

Sniffing is how dogs read the news and bring their pulse down — a scent 'decompression walk' is the kindest thing you can offer a shelter dog learning about walks. It also teaches your dog the other half of the leash deal: 'slack leash = you may smell whatever you like, forever'.

What you need

  • A 15–30 ft long line clipped to a well-fitted harness
  • A quiet field, park quiet-zone or safe backyard
  • A pocket of treats for check-ins

Common mistakes

  • This isn't an obedience workout — every 'heel' during decompression costs the whole point.
  • Don't drag past every hedge; the lesson is 'out here, your body settles', not 'we cover distance'.
  • No retractable leads — a dropped handle sets the dog loose, and constant tension teaches pulling. Use the fixed long line and follow its safety rules.

If your dog is struggling

They're too hyped to actually sniff.

Shorter and quieter wins — try 10 minutes at dawn, and seed the first 30 seconds by scattering a few kibble bits in the grass yourself; the nose usually takes over.

The long line keeps tangling around my legs.

Wear it back-tied over one shoulder, keep your thumb out of the loop, and stand still more than you walk — most of a sniffari is you waiting while your dog explores.
