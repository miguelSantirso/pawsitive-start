Why this matters

Many rescues have car histories you'll never know: transport cages, one-way trips, a history of fear. Cars matter for vet visits and emergencies, so we rebuild them slow: parked car = snack place, engine on = nothing happens, one-minute drives = micro-adventures.

What you need

  • Treats
  • A crash-tested harness or secured crate
  • A stuffed chew
  • Short destination list (park! NOT the vet first)

Mastery check

You have got this when all of these are true in a normal session, on a normal day.

Common mistakes

  • Don't drive with them loose in the cabin or poking out the front window — restraint is safety AND calm.
  • Don't make the first destination the clinic; anxious associations from day one linger for months.
  • Don't scold drool or crying — pace, treat placement and shorter trips are the medicine.

If your dog is struggling

They refuse to load.

Back several rungs and park the car for three days with treats inside only when nobody pushes; some dogs need weeks of 'car = neutral' before 'car = good'. Severe, shaking refusal = a force-free behaviorist; motion sickness is also treatable — ask the vet.

Jumping straight back out.

Feed the stuffed toy ON the seat while YOU sit in the back; build 'stay in the seat' with treats BEFORE the engine ever turns.
