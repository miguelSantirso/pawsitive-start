Why this matters

Shelter dogs inherit unknown handling histories, and vet-visit bites often start with subtle freezing nobody noticed. Cooperative care flips the script: your dog controls a 'start button' (chin staying in your palm = continue, lifting it = pause), and every handling step gets paid. Calm exams become more likely with practice.

What you need

  • A brush and nail clippers (out but unused at first)
  • Dog toothpaste
  • Treats
  • Both hands

Common mistakes

  • Never hold a dog down 'so they learn' — cooperative care exists to replace exactly that.
  • Don't proceed past the chin-up pause; the button means 'stop' every single time.
  • No nail trims in these early games — that frame (holding + cutting) comes later, with separate skills and a pro if needed.

If your dog is struggling

A certain zone isn't okay (paws flipped or ears).

Slide from an already-happy zone one inch per session toward the tricky one — give treats each step; Fear Free-style 'gradual approach to sensitive zones' takes longer for some rescues but it always pays.

They freeze, stiffen hard, back away completely.

That's a hard-no, not a stubborn moment: end the session, and set up a vet visit; frozen dogs don't learn from pressure, they learn from letting them watch good things at their own distance.
