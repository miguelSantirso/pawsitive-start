The goalDoorways hold your dog until you release them — front door, back door, car door: the anti-dash exercise.

Why this matters

Door-darting is a common way dogs get lost, and bolting is also rehearsed in indoor doorways before it ever becomes a street accident. This is impulse control with real stakes — and it's taught entirely with waiting, never with holding or forcing.

What you need

  • Indoor doors first
  • Treats
  • A gate at the exterior door while unproven

Common mistakes

  • Never slam the door in alarm at a bolt — calm, boring resets keep 'wait' a fair game instead of a thrill.
  • Don't rely on this skill for real safety yet — at the first real kid-opens-the-door moment, a gate is what saves a life.
  • Don't practice only with boring times — proof a little at 'busy moments' once indoors goes well.

If your dog is struggling

They bolt anyway at the front door.

Use the backup plan: baby-gate or a shut door at the exterior route every single time, and keep rebuilding in the hallway. Safety is management, and 'wait' is trained at calmer doors first.

They sit but the moment I touch the handle… gone.

Split the picture in half: touch the handle, pay for sitting, take your hand OFF, pay again. Then the same with a 1 cm door opening. Handle-touch becomes its own boring event.
